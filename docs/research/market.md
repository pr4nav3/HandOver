HandOver market and competitor research (as of 2026-11-14)

**Answer first:** Most competitors do one of two things. Some turn an expert's video into a document (text steps, sometimes a quiz). Others check work with cameras, but only after a station camera is installed and recorded work cycles are labeled. Free live AI (Gemini Live, Meta Live AI) watches through your camera but has no expert content, so it invents steps. HandOver's gap is the closed loop: the expert's own clip is the instruction, and the learner's camera decides when the next clip plays. It needs one take, no labeling, and any device with a browser.

**How I sourced this:** "Fetched" means I opened the page today. "Search" means I only read a search result summary today, so treat it as inferred. I added nothing from memory.

**Competitors: what they do and what they lack**
- **DeepSight (Montreal):** Records experienced operators wearing smart glasses. AI turns the recordings into step-by-step digital work instructions, usually delivered on tablets. Reports 30 to 40% less training time. Smart-glasses conversation is a long-term goal, not shipped. Lacks: no live check of the learner; the output is a document. (Search: https://agfundernews.com/deepsight-takes-augmented-reality-to-the-factory-floor, https://agroempresario.com/publicacion/115532/deepsight-brings-augmented-reality-and-ai-onto-the-factory-floor/)
- **Retrocausal Assembly Copilot:** Real-time step tracking with alerts, signal towers and links to factory line systems. Setup has four steps: write the procedure, capture a few cycles, label one cycle, deploy a trained model. The camera must be placed "such that it is able to observe all the steps." Lacks: needs a fixed station, a labeling pass and a trained model; it shows no expert video. (Fetched: https://retrocausal.ai)
- **Dozuki CreatorPro:** "Video to Guide" and "Document to Guide." Outputs numbered text steps plus a quiz. A separate "VisualVerify AI" examines captured images of completed work. That is the closest overlap with HandOver, but it checks photos after the work, not a live step-by-step loop. Lacks: the instruction is text, and it does not play the expert's clip. (Fetched: https://dozuki.com/creatorpro)
- **VSight VideoFlow:** AI splits uploaded video into timestamped steps, with an editor to fix the timestamps. Lacks: no camera check of the learner. Note that automatic step cutting by itself is not unique to HandOver. (Search: https://vsight.io/blog/introducing-videoflow-revolutionizing-training-and-operational-guidance/)
- **Tulip AI Composer + Vision:** Composer turns a PDF procedure (SOP) into a no-code app. Launched April 2025, open to all users fall 2025. A third-party listing says it accepts PDF only and generates limited app logic. Tulip Vision does inspection and step checks at configured stations. Lacks: starts from documents, not an expert's take; vision checks need station setup. (Search: https://tulip.co/press/ai-composer-announcement/, https://tulip.co/platform/artificial-intelligence)
- **Augmentir:** Connected-worker platform. Its "Augie" assistant turns informal tribal knowledge into SOPs, and it tailors instructions to each worker's skill level. All claims are from the vendor. Lacks: text and workflow output, no camera-gated video steps. (Search: https://augmentir.com/product)
- **Poka (owned by IFS since June 2023):** Training, skills and troubleshooting platform for factory workers; more than 1,000 manufacturers. Lacks: no live verification found. (Search: https://www.ifs.com/news/corporate/ifs-acquires-poka)
- **Scribe:** Records your clicks in a browser or desktop app and produces a guide with annotated screenshots. Free plan exists. Lacks: screen tasks only, nothing physical. (Search: https://scribe.com/tools/step-by-step-guide-generator)
- **Microsoft Dynamics 365 Guides (HoloLens):** "Dynamics 365 Guides and Remote Assist will no longer be available after December 31, 2026." Microsoft has announced no successor (search: Directions on Microsoft). This leaves a real gap about 7 weeks out. (Fetched: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/mixed-reality/guides/known-issues)
- **TeamViewer Frontline:** Remote expert assistance with AR overlays, plus AiStudio for training image recognition. On-device Windows AI video enhancement was in closed beta in June 2026. Lacks: built around a live remote expert, not a recorded expert. (Search: https://www.teamviewer.com/ru/global/company/press/2026/teamviewer-windows-ai-assist-ar-frontline-workers/)
- **Apple Vision Pro apps (Taqtile Manifest, BILT, Scope AR WorkLink):** Authored 3D, animated and text guides. Lacks: costly headset, authored content (not captured from an expert's take), Apple hardware only. (Search: https://biltapp.com/press-articles/bilt-launches-immersive-guidance-for-apple-vision-pro, https://www.scopear.com/resources/scope-ar-releases-worklink-app-for-apple-vision-pro)
- **Ray-Ban Meta "Live AI":** Session-based; the camera stays on and you can ask for a recipe walkthrough. At its public demo it said the chef had already done steps he had not done. Lacks: no expert ground truth; it makes steps up. (Search: https://www.meta.com/help/ai-glasses/894093646030348/, https://spilled.gg/metas-ai-smart-glasses-demo-fails-assistant-starts-describing-cooking-steps-havent-happened-yet)
- **Google Gemini Live:** Camera and screen sharing free on Android and iOS since May 2025. General assistant with no step state and no expert content. (Search: https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/gemini-live-is-now-free-for-everyone-on-android-and-ios-and-you-can-finally-share-your-screen-and-camera-on-iphone)
- **YouTube how-to:** The expert's real hands and voice, but the learner pauses and scrubs by hand, and nothing checks their progress. HandOver is effectively "YouTube that waits for you." (Inferred; no source needed.)

**Sharpest one-sentence differentiation**
- "HandOver turns one narrated take by an expert into a lesson that plays their own clip for each step and only moves on when the learner's camera shows the step is done: no station camera, no labeling, no headset required."

**Market need evidence**
- **Manufacturing hiring:** Up to 3.8 million new manufacturing workers needed in 2024 to 2033. "5 in 10 or 1.9 million of these jobs could go unfilled." The page mentions retiring Baby Boomers but gives no retirement count. (Fetched: https://www.themanufacturinginstitute.org/manufacturers-need-as-many-as-3-8-million-new-employees-by-2033/, Deloitte and Manufacturing Institute, April 3, 2024)
- **Construction:** Needs 439,000 net new workers in 2025 and up to 499,000 in 2026. The median construction worker is now under 42, meaning "an increasingly large section of the workforce lacks experience." In short: lots of novices, few experts. (Search: https://www.constructiondive.com/news/2025-workers-construction-needs-how-many/738205/)
- **Aging:** Over 4.1 million Americans turn 65 each year through 2027 ("Peak 65," Alliance for Lifetime Income), about 11,200 a day. (Search: https://www.aarp.org/money/retirement/silver-tsunami-late-boomers-turn-65/)
- **Training cost:** ATD reports $1,283 per employee in 2023 and $1,254 in 2024; cost per learning hour rose from $123 to $165. (Search: https://assets.td.org/m/483691e33fbdf4db/original/State_of_the_Industry_PDF_Download_2025.pdf)
- **Caution:** One analysis of government labor data finds only about 18% of electricians are 55 or older, so the "boomer exodus in the trades" story is weaker than headlines say. Frame it as "demand outpaces experienced people," not "everyone is retiring." Ignore blog figures like "4 million trades by 2030"; I could not trace them to a source. (Search: https://startbusinessbystate.com/?p=23969)

**Cross-platform angle (relevant to the every-OS goal)**
- The incumbents are each tied to one device family: HoloLens (retiring), Vision Pro, Meta glasses, or a fixed station camera. HandOver's capture page runs in a phone browser and the learn screen runs in a laptop browser, so Android to Windows, iPhone to Mac, and a Mac-made lesson on Windows all work through the same web stack. Normalizing video to H.264 is what makes Windows playback work. That is an honest difference, but claim only the device pairs actually tested.
- From a Mac you can check: iOS Safari capture, the macOS learn screen, and Chrome on macOS. Without hardware you cannot check: Android Chrome capture (needs a real Android phone or an emulator from the free Android SDK) and Windows playback and speech input (needs a Windows machine; Edge and Chrome on Windows behave differently).

**Three honest positioning lines**
1. "Record it once while you explain it. HandOver cuts your take into steps and plays the right clip, in your voice, when the learner's camera shows they're ready."
2. "Like a how-to video that waits for you: it watches your hands and plays the next step only when the last one is done."
3. "Expert video plus camera checks, with no station to install and no cycles to label. A phone and a laptop on any OS are enough."

**Claims to avoid**
- Do not say "first" or "only." VSight already splits video into steps, Dozuki's VisualVerify checks images of finished work, and Retrocausal tracks steps live.
- Do not quote training-time savings. HandOver has no measured results, and the 30 to 40% figure is DeepSight's own claim.
- Say "one take" only if the lesson really came from one take.
